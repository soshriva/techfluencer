# Article Title

## Writing Style Rules

Use normal human English while writing blogs.

Avoid wording patterns that make the article look AI-generated. In particular:

- Do not overuse em dashes.
- Prefer commas, periods, or shorter sentences where they sound more natural.
- Avoid inflated or overly polished phrasing.
- Avoid generic transitions that sound automated.
- Keep the tone clear, practical, and conversational.
- Write like an experienced practitioner explaining the concept to another practitioner.
- Use simple words where possible, but keep technical terms when they are needed.

Example:

- Avoid: My career spans BMC Software, VMware, Credit Suisse, and Broadcom — giving me a practical view of enterprise platforms.
- Prefer: My career spans BMC Software, VMware, Credit Suisse, and Broadcom, giving me a practical view of enterprise platforms.

## Visual Style Standard

All primary article visuals should use a consistent **neon dark-mode enterprise technology infographic** style.

This style may also be described as:

- Futuristic neon infographic
- Dark-mode technology infographic
- Premium enterprise-cloud explainer graphic
- Dashboard-style technical infographic
- Glassmorphism and HUD-inspired visual design

### Visual Characteristics

- Use a deep navy or near-black background.
- Use bright cyan, electric blue, purple, green, and amber accents.
- Use subtle neon glows, gradients, illuminated borders, and glassmorphism panels.
- Use polished enterprise-cloud icons and simple architectural symbols.
- Organise content into clearly separated sections, panels, or process stages.
- Use strong visual hierarchy with one clear title and concise supporting labels.
- Use arrows and connectors to make relationships and traffic flows immediately understandable.
- Keep the composition premium, modern, memorable, and suitable for LinkedIn sharing.
- Prefer a wide 16:9 layout, normally 1600 × 900 pixels.
- Export final website visuals as high-resolution PNG files.

### Accuracy Rules for Visuals

- Visual appeal must never override technical correctness.
- Validate every label, arrow, analogy, and relationship against official product or upstream documentation.
- Treat analogies as memory aids, not one-to-one technical definitions.
- Add a short qualifier when an analogy could otherwise create a false technical conclusion.
- Avoid absolute wording when platform behaviour is conditional, eventual, implementation-dependent, or policy-dependent.
- Distinguish configuration objects from the controllers or implementations that act on them.
- Distinguish Kubernetes API objects from the underlying infrastructure assets they represent.
- Keep text concise enough to remain readable at normal browser width.

### Standard Image Prompt

Use the following prompt as the default starting point for article visuals:

> Create a premium neon dark-mode enterprise technology infographic on a deep navy background. Use glowing cyan, electric blue, purple, green, and amber accents, glassmorphism panels, subtle gradients, crisp enterprise-cloud icons, illuminated connectors, and a polished dashboard-style layout. Present the concept in a clear 16:9 composition with strong hierarchy, concise labels, high contrast, and technically accurate relationships. The result should feel modern, memorable, professional, and suitable for a technical blog and LinkedIn sharing.

### Avoid

- Flat or generic corporate clip art
- Cartoonish illustrations
- Excessive decorative effects that reduce readability
- Dense paragraphs inside images
- Low-contrast text
- Unverified technical labels
- Misleading arrows or one-to-one mappings
- Images that look visually impressive but teach the wrong concept

## 6. Knowledge Check

Knowledge checks must be interactive and follow the same standard used in Blog 001 and Blog 002.

For every question:

- Show four visible answer options.
- Let the reader select one option.
- Do not show the explanation before selection.
- After selection, reveal the answer review.
- Clearly identify the correct answer.
- Explain why the correct option is correct.
- Explain why each wrong option is wrong.
- Treat the knowledge check as learning reinforcement, not grading.
